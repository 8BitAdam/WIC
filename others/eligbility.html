Eligbility -- chnge who Qualifies

Changing who qualifies for WIC is different from changing some numbers or making the application easier. WIC is based on four Major criteria credibility, housing, income and of course nutritional risk (U.S. Department of Agriculture, 2024). While these requirements cover important parts of families situations they still often leave families outside cutoffs where they do not fit neatly into requirements. My proposal is to make general hardship a stronger part of determining eligibility instead of just a list of hard requirements. This would allow a family that's can demo issues affording adequate nutritious food can have hardship considered alongside its other requirements

  • Age
    • Riase childs max age from 5th to 16th birthday
  • Income
    • Switch from Net into to gross income
  • Postpartum
    • Expand the time beyond current limits
  • instabilty
    • take into account historical instabilty, and future instabilty

This could be down without heavily changing the current system. WIC could make additional eligibility pathways for households that could document food insecurities, expand income, age, postpartum and significant unavoidable expanses like medical bills. Research on WIC participation has found that logistics, staff interactions, knowledge and awareness Figueroa et al. (2025). Figueroa reviewed 33 U.S. studies published between 2019 and 2024 and found that repeated factors were associated with whether eligible people participated in WIC, particularly program logistics and staff were found to be the most common barrier while knowledge and awareness were the most common individual factors. Rosemary hunter associated with the Vanderbilt University Medic Center found that 88% of healthcare providers surveyed healthcare providers and 90% were familiar with WIC but only 39% made direct WIC referrals. While these studies them self do not show eligibility is does show there is a want or a need for being in the program.
